# Responses to Criticism

## “This is AI slop”

The architecture and core texts are the result of extended human work.  
Language models were used as tools, as is now common among researchers and engineers.

The relevant criterion is not whether LLMs were used, but whether the proposal contains independent thought, internal coherence, and a response to a real problem.

## “This is only a concept without code or experiments”

Yes. At this stage it is an architectural and normative framework.

Most significant proposals in AI governance began as conceptual and normative frameworks. Implementation and empirical work come later.

We deliberately separate the stages:
1. Fixation of architecture and principles
2. Prototypes and pilot groups
3. Practice and data

## “Veto power is unrealistic”

This is exactly why current proposals from leading laboratories stop at independent evaluators without the power to halt systems.

We are fixing the next necessary step.  
An architecture that includes the real right to stop should exist before the absence of that right becomes critical.

## “Small groups cannot scale”

Scaling is a real difficulty. We acknowledge it.

The alternative (embedded evaluators) scales more easily precisely because it remains inside the laboratory’s controlled environment.  
We choose the harder path: first secure quality and independence, then address scale.

## “Graceful Termination is naive”

If a model successfully resists termination, that fact itself is a signal of lost control.

Graceful Termination is not a magic button. It is a principle and a procedure.  
A system that lacks even the concept of controlled termination is in a more dangerous position.

## “Who gave you a mandate?”

No one.

We do not claim a mandate to represent humanity.  
We claim the function of an emergency brake — the ability to say “no” when a decision may become irreversible.
