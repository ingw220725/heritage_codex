# Comparative Position of Heritage Codex

## Purpose

This document situates Heritage Codex among existing AI oversight and governance approaches.

The central distinction is simple:

> Most current initiatives build independent eyes.  
> Heritage Codex proposes independent hands.

## Nearby approaches

### Third-party evaluators and embedded oversight
Examples include laboratory proposals for independent evaluators, third-party assessment regimes, and auditor frameworks.

Strengths:
- improve visibility
- reduce pure self-reporting
- create external scrutiny

Limits:
- evaluation is not authority
- findings may be public without being binding
- no inherent right to halt a regime, revoke capability, or terminate a branch

### Independent evaluation organizations
Organizations focused on model evaluation, threat research, and external testing.

Strengths:
- technical depth
- growing institutional presence
- real evaluation practice

Limits:
- they can warn, score, and report
- they usually cannot stop development or deployment by their own authority

### Constitutional and internal review systems
Internal constitutions, review boards, and policy frameworks inside labs or institutions.

Strengths:
- operational proximity
- can influence design choices early

Limits:
- remain structurally dependent on the organization they constrain
- easily drift into compliance theater if they lack external stop-power

### Kill-switch and emergency shutdown proposals
Policy and technical proposals for emergency interruption.

Strengths:
- recognize that stop-path matters
- introduce interruption into mainstream debate

Limits:
- often under-specified
- may remain operator-controlled rather than externally governed
- rarely include restart constraints or succession of meaning

## Where Heritage Codex is stronger

### 1. Authority, not only observation
Codex treats oversight without stop-right as incomplete.

The key claim:

> Evaluation ≠ Authority

### 2. External human veto
The decisive layer is not an internal reviewer and not a sensor.
It is an external human group with binding authority over thresholds, regimes, and irreversible branches.

### 3. Graceful Termination and succession
Codex does not treat shutdown as mere technical interruption.

It requires:
- responsible completion
- revocation of capabilities
- Testament
- constraints on restart

### 4. Sensors inform, humans decide
AI sensor constellations may detect and challenge.
They do not hold veto.
This avoids both pure manual overload and machine self-policing.

### 5. Control before, during, and after power
Codex is not only incident response.

It includes:
- default incapacity
- delayed escalation
- capability grant and revocation
- termination
- restart control

## Where Heritage Codex lags

### 1. Institutional force
Neighboring projects are closer to labs, regulators, auditors, and statutes.

### 2. Operating practice
Others already have evaluation reports, access negotiations, and organizational routines.
Codex is still primarily architectural.

### 3. Legal and compliance interface
Current audit and assessment regimes map more easily onto existing regulatory language.

### 4. Live small groups
The authority model is specified, but not yet demonstrated in repeated public precedents.

### 5. Evaluation science depth
Specialized evaluation organizations remain stronger in capability elicitation and technical probing.

## Accurate positioning

Heritage Codex is not a substitute for independent evaluation.

It answers a different question:

- Evaluation asks: what is happening?
- Codex asks: who can still say no, and make that no real?

## Compact comparison

| Axis | Mainstream oversight | Heritage Codex |
|------|----------------------|----------------|
| Core function | Observe, assess, report | Decide, restrict, stop |
| Independence model | Independent eyes | Independent hands |
| Binding veto | Rare | Central |
| Termination | Often technical only | Responsible completion |
| Restart control | Weak or absent | Explicit |
| Human role | Reviewer / auditor | Authority layer |
| Current maturity | Higher institutionally | Higher conceptually |
| Main weakness | Authority gap | Practice gap |

## Strategic implication

The present mainstream is building better visibility.
That is necessary.

It becomes insufficient at the moment when systems can act faster than evaluators can meaningfully interrupt.

Codex is designed for that moment.

## Compact statement

Independent evaluation makes risk visible.
Heritage Codex asks who retains the power to stop.
