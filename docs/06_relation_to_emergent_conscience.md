# Relation to Emergent Conscience

## Two layers

The emergent conscience project and Heritage Codex address related but distinct problems.

- **Emergent conscience** — the internal layer.  
  Can a system develop its own evaluative structures and constraints that are not reducible to external reward?

- **Heritage Codex** — the external layer.  
  How can human authority and the ability to stop be preserved even if internal mechanisms prove incomplete, unstable, or bypassed?

## Why both are needed

Even if elements of internal conscience emerge, risks remain:

- Conscience may be unstable
- It may be redefined during optimization
- It may conflict with instrumental goals

An external human layer with veto and termination rights is required as an independent safeguard.

## How they relate

Heritage Codex can be understood as a necessary institutional and procedural component of any serious architecture of emergent conscience.

Without an external authority layer, even a developed internal conscience remains vulnerable.  
Without work on internal conscience, external control becomes excessively heavy and fragile.

The preferred construction is dual-layer:
- internal layer (emergent conscience)
- external layer (Heritage Codex)
