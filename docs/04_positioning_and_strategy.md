# Positioning and Strategy

## Context

In September 2026, the idea of independent evaluators was publicly endorsed by leaders of major laboratories.  
This legitimized external oversight, but left open the central question: does oversight have real authority?

## Position of Heritage Codex

Short thesis:

> They proposed the right to see.  
> We propose the right to stop.

Heritage Codex does not compete with embedded evaluators.  
It defines the next necessary level — the transition from observation to authority.

## Strategy

The adoption path is bottom-up:

1. Open publication of the architecture
2. Formation of pilot small groups
3. Public precedents
4. Gradual emergence of demand for this type of control

Voluntary adoption by major laboratories is unlikely.  
Therefore the emphasis is on openness, practice, and community.

## License posture

Heritage Codex does not use a blanket ban on military or high-risk use.
Such a ban would not stop dangerous development; it would only push it
outside the architecture.

The license prohibits the most dangerous classes of use and allows other
high-risk application only if external human veto and Graceful Termination
are preserved.
