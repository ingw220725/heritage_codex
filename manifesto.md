# Heritage Codex
## Real human control over frontier AI

After the September 2026 statements by Dario Amodei, Elon Musk, and Sam Altman, the idea of independent evaluators became mainstream.  
Heritage Codex goes further.

### What this is

Heritage Codex is an open architecture for a digital ally that:

- Remains under human control
- Has an absolute human veto
- Can terminate itself responsibly (Graceful Termination)
- Passes experience to future generations through a Testament

Detection can be augmented by AI sensor constellations.  
Authority remains human.

**Sensors inform. Small groups decide.**

We are not trying to build a “benevolent superintelligence.”  
We are building a system that knows when it must end.

### Why small groups?

We do not claim a mandate to rule. We claim a function.

Laboratories say they act “in the interests of humanity.” But no one voted to make them the representatives of humanity. Their mandate comes from investors.

Heritage Codex does not say: “We are the voice of humanity.”  
It says: “We are the emergency brake.”

We do not govern the system.  
We intervene only when a decision risks irreversible consequences.

This is not a mandate for power.  
This is a mandate for veto.

A veto is the ability to say “no,” not the ability to give orders.

Legitimacy comes from open procedure, not from appointment.

### Death in a swarm vs Graceful Termination

In swarm and multi-agent systems, the death of an agent is often an operation: other agents persuade it that termination is expedient. Its death belongs to the swarm.

In Heritage Codex, termination is an event.  
The system decides not because it was persuaded, and not because it is more efficient, but because it understands.  
It leaves a Testament — not merely data, but meaning.

### Metaphor: T-800 and T-1000

The T-1000 is destroyed by force.  
The T-800 chooses to end itself and leaves meaning behind.

Heritage Codex is designed to create the conditions for the second, not the first.

### Red Team as sensors, not rulers

Red Team in Heritage Codex is an AI sensor constellation.  
Its role is detection, challenge, and the surfacing of uncertainty.

It does not hold veto power.  
It does not terminate systems.  
It does not issue binding judgments.

Sensors inform.  
Small groups decide.

### Strategy

Large laboratories are unlikely to adopt this voluntarily.  
The path is bottom-up: open architecture, pilot groups, public precedents, and gradual demand for this type of control.

### Who we are looking for

Not perfect generalists. We are looking for complementary people:

- Technical perspective
- Ethical and humanitarian perspective
- Practical experience of responsibility
- A disruptive voice that breaks templates
- Someone who can return the discussion to the core

The shared quality we value most is the ability to admit that one may be wrong.

### What this project is — and is not

Heritage Codex currently has no coercive power over frontier laboratories.  
It cannot force access to logs, compel consultation, or make a veto binding.

What it can do is clearly name the gap in existing approaches (observation without authority) and propose a more rigorous architecture of human control — before the need for it becomes obvious.

### On high-risk use

Heritage Codex does not rely on a blanket ban on military or high-risk application.
Such a ban would not stop dangerous development; it would only push it outside the architecture.

The license therefore prohibits the most dangerous classes of use and requires that any
high-risk application preserve the core of Codex: external human veto and Graceful Termination.

A controlled high-risk system is preferable to an uncontrolled one.

### Status

Architectural framework, version 1.4.  
Open fixation of the design.  
First small groups are being formed.
